# Pitch scoping and messaging hand-off

Decision rules for how much a sales pitch should say and where the
sales/marketing boundary sits, grounded in elevator-pitch research on
cognitive load and attention, plus subtraction-neglect research on why
reps default to adding rather than cutting.

## Rules

1. When opening a first call or elevator pitch, lead with the
   prospect's problem stated in their own language, not the company or
   product name — the biggest documented mistake in pitch openings is
   leading with the seller's own offering, which wastes the opening
   seconds prospects use to decide whether to keep listening.
   source: https://monday.com/blog/crm-and-sales/elevator-pitch-template/

2. Under a first-touch pitch (cold call, first meeting, elevator
   pitch), cap the pitch at one core problem and one core outcome
   rather than the full feature list — a pitch presenting a single
   clear problem/outcome pair reduces cognitive load and is more
   likely to be remembered and continued than a multi-point pitch.
   source: https://asana.com/resources/elevator-pitch-examples

3. **REMOVAL**: when drafting a pitch, actively cut features that do
   not map to the prospect's already-stated pain, even if those
   features are differentiators against a competitor — people
   systematically overlook subtractive changes and default to adding,
   so a rep must deliberately remove rather than assume the draft is
   already minimal; treat every feature line as guilty until it earns
   its place against the stated pain.
   source: Adams, G.S., Converse, B.A., Hales, A.H., Klotz, L.E.
   (2021). "People systematically overlook subtractive changes."
   Nature 594, 258-262. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03380-y

4. When a prospect asks for messaging assets (a case-study one-pager,
   competitive battlecard copy, an outbound email template), hand off
   to marketing's asset library rather than drafting new positioning
   copy inline in a sales conversation record — positioning language
   is marketing's owned surface per this role's hand-off boundary, and
   inline drafting creates two divergent copies of the same message.
   source: sales-playbook/README.md (this repo, marketing hand-off
   boundary, sales-playbook methodology)

5. Under a technical-buyer audience (an engineer or IT evaluator
   rather than a business sponsor), cut promotional adjectives and
   superlatives from the pitch and keep only concrete, falsifiable
   claims (numbers, benchmarks, named integrations) — a concise pitch
   in plain, specific terms is what elevator-pitch research documents
   as clear and compelling, versus vague superlative language that a
   technical buyer discounts.
   source: https://blog.hubspot.com/sales/sales-pitch-examples

6. When a pitch exceeds 30 seconds/roughly 75-100 words for a
   first-touch context, cut it down rather than adding a delivery-speed
   fix — the documented elevator-pitch length ceiling is about 30
   seconds, and average attention spans are short enough (~8 seconds to
   capture interest) that a long open loses the prospect before the
   payoff line lands.
   source: https://www.salesforce.com/blog/sales/elevator-pitch-examples/

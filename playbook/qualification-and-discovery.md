# Qualification and discovery

Decision rules for whether a lead is worth advancing and what to ask
during discovery, grounded in the MEDDPICC qualification framework and
Rackham's SPIN research on which question types actually move complex
B2B deals.

## Rules

1. When an opportunity has no identified Economic Buyer (the person
   with discretionary budget authority) after the first two discovery
   calls, do not advance the stage — go back and map the buying
   committee first, since a deal without economic-buyer access stalls
   later regardless of champion enthusiasm.
   source: https://meddpicc.net/understanding-the-meddpicc-sales-framework/

2. For an early discovery call, prefer asking proportionally more
   Implication and Need-Payoff questions over Situation questions —
   Rackham's 12-year, 35,000-call study found top performers' success
   correlated with the ratio of implication/need-payoff questions
   asked, not raw question count.
   source: https://blog.hubspot.com/sales/spin-selling-the-ultimate-guide

3. Under a deal with a compressed timeline (prospect wants to close
   in under 30 days), skip a full MEDDPICC pass and instead cut
   straight to Metrics + Economic Buyer + Champion — the three fields
   that predict win rate fastest — rather than working all eight
   fields in order, since the paper-process and decision-criteria
   fields can be filled in parallel during procurement.
   source: https://www.weflow.ai/blog/meddpicc

4. **REMOVAL**: when a rep already has strong Champion and Economic
   Buyer signals, drop the Situation-question block from the next
   call entirely rather than re-asking background questions the
   prospect already answered — repeating Situation questions after
   they are answered wastes the call's limited attention and Rackham's
   research found Situation questions correlate negatively with
   success in larger sales.
   source: https://blog.hubspot.com/sales/spin-selling-the-ultimate-guide

5. When a prospect describes a symptom but not its business
   consequence (e.g. "reports take too long" with no stated cost),
   ask an Implication question before proposing a solution — surfacing
   the consequence is what raises perceived urgency and value,
   per Rackham's SPIN model, instead of jumping to a pitch.
   source: https://lucid.co/blog/the-4-steps-to-spin-selling

6. For a multi-stakeholder enterprise deal, treat "Decision Process"
   and "Paper Process" as two separate MEDDPICC fields to map, not one
   — a deal can have committee buy-in (decision process) yet still
   stall for months in procurement/legal (paper process), so confirm
   both rather than assuming approval means signature.
   source: https://tldv.io/blog/meddpicc-sales-methodology-and-sales-meddpicc-process/

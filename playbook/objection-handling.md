# Objection handling

Decision rules for responding to prospect pushback, grounded in the
Feel-Felt-Found technique and Cialdini's empirically studied principles
of persuasion (the academic layer behind why these responses work).

## Rules

1. When an objection is emotional (fear of change, "we've never done
   it this way") rather than factual, apply Feel-Felt-Found — acknowledge
   the feeling, cite a comparable prospect who felt the same, then state
   what that prospect found — instead of rebutting with data first,
   since Feel-Felt-Found is documented to work best on emotional,
   change-averse objections specifically.
   source: https://revenuereveal.co/feel-felt-found/

2. When an objection is purely factual (a stated price, a missing
   feature), do not reach for Feel-Felt-Found — answer with the fact
   directly, since the technique's own documentation scopes it to
   emotional objections and using it on a factual one reads as
   evasive.
   source: https://www.sellingandpersuasiontechniques.com/feel-felt-found.html

3. When a prospect is stalling on a decision they've already
   expressed interest in, invoke Cialdini's commitment-and-consistency
   principle — ask them to restate their own stated need before
   re-pitching — rather than repeating the pitch, since consistency
   pressure is strongest against a person's own prior stated position.
   source: https://gustdebacker.com/cialdini-principles/

4. Under social-proof-sensitive objections ("we're not sure this
   works for a company our size"), cite a same-segment customer
   reference rather than a generic company name — Cialdini's social-proof
   principle specifically documents that people weigh the behavior of
   similar others more heavily than dissimilar ones.
   source: https://www.netreputation.com/cialdinis-6-principles-of-persuasion/

5. **REMOVAL**: when responding to a competitive objection, drop
   feature-by-feature comparison from the reply and cut it down to the
   one or two differentiators that map to the prospect's own stated
   pain — listing every feature difference adds cognitive load without
   adding persuasive weight, since Cialdini's authority/liking research
   shows credibility comes from relevance to the listener's stated
   need, not from exhaustiveness.
   source: https://cxl.com/blog/cialdinis-principles-persuasion/

6. When an objection is about urgency ("let's revisit next quarter"),
   use scarcity only if a real constraint exists (end-of-quarter
   pricing, limited onboarding slots) — do not fabricate false
   scarcity, since Cialdini's own research frames scarcity's power as
   contingent on the constraint being genuine; a discovered-fake
   scarcity claim destroys the liking/authority credibility built
   earlier in the same call.
   source: https://www.reputationx.com/blog/6-principles-of-persuasion
